Owner Guide

Everything you need to do each month, in order.

The short version. Once a month: open the month, approve availability, enter orders, assign people, sort deliveries and strawberry runs. After the month: confirm hours, run salary, invoice clients, then read the summary to see what you actually made.

{# ── 1 · One-time setup ──────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
1

One-time setup

Do this once when you first start
Change the owner password

The default password is ichigo-admin — change it before sharing the app with anyone.

Input → Owner password card → type a new password → Change

Add your employees

Each employee needs a name and a PIN (4+ digits) to log in.

Input → Employees card → fill in name + PIN → Add employee
Share each person's name + PIN with them directly. They can change their PIN after logging in.

Add your clients

Clients are the restaurants and cafes you sell to.

Input → Clients card → enter client name → Add

Set pay rates

These drive the salary calculation. Check they're correct.

Input → Pay rate card
• Piece rate — dollars earned per daifuku produced (currently ${{ '%.2f'|format(piece_rate) }})
• Gusto base rate — the hourly rate used to convert total pay into Gusto hours (currently ${{ '%.2f'|format(gusto_rate) }})
• Strawberry price — reimbursed for each purchase run (currently ${{ '%.2f'|format(strawberry_price) }})
• Delivery transport — the per-stop delivery fee (currently ${{ '%.2f'|format(delivery_transport) }})

Set what each client pays you

These are net to you per piece, with any revenue split already applied — not the client's shelf price.

Summary → Sales table → edit the Rate column → Save prices
The same rate is used on the Invoice page.

Channel rates live on the same page: pop-up ${{ '%.2f'|format(popup_rate) }} less {{ '%g'|format(popup_waste * 100) }}% waste, pick-up ${{ '%.2f'|format(pickup_rate) }}.

Share the schedule link (optional)

Anyone with this link can see the calendar without logging in — handy for a staff group chat.

Calendar → Generate link → Copy. Revoke kills the old link immediately.

{# ── 2 · Open the month ──────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
2

Open the month

~3–4 weeks before the month starts
Generate the shift dates

Input → Generate month → pick the month → Generate

This creates every Mon/Tue/Wed/Fri production date from your shift templates. Safe to run twice — dates that already exist are left alone, and it tells you how many were added.

Tell the team to submit availability

Employees log in and tick the shifts they can work, plus delivery days and strawberry purchase runs — all on one page, one submission.

They go to Availability → tick shifts → Save availability.

Delete a date you don't need

Input → find the shift row → Delete
Removes that day's assignments and availability too. Also available on the Calendar.

{# ── 3 · Approve availability ────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
3

Check submissions

As submissions come in
Nothing to approve

Availability counts the moment an employee submits it — there's no approval step. They can change it themselves any time before the shift.

See who's free on a day from the Schedule or the shift's own page.

Clear any conflicts

A conflict means someone is assigned to a shift they never said they could work — usually because they edited their availability after you assigned them.

Approvals → Remove all conflicting assignments, then reassign those days.

{# ── 4 · Enter orders ────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
4

Enter client orders

As orders are confirmed
Add the orders for a day

Calendar → 📋 Orders on the day → choose client, enter quantities per flavour → Add order

Order quantities drive everything downstream: how many people the app suggests, what each shift pays, and what you invoice.

Deliver on a different day

Set Deliver on if the order is made one day and delivered another. The calendar and invoices use the delivery date.

Mark pop-ups

Tick Pop-up for stock you sell yourself at a market or event. Pop-up pieces skip delivery entirely and are priced at the pop-up rate.

Repeating orders

Recurring → set a standing weekly order per client, then apply it to a month instead of typing it out each week.

{# ── 5 · Assign people ───────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
5

Assign people to shifts

Once availability is approved
See the whole month at once

Schedule → pick the month

One row per shift showing orders, suggested team size, who's assigned and whether coverage is in band — plus every delivery day underneath. Start here, then open whichever day needs work.

Open a shift

Schedule → Assign on the row, or Calendar → 👥 Staff on the day

The page shows how many people the orders justify, who is available, and whether the team you've picked covers the shift.

Let the app pick, then adjust

⚡ Smart auto-assign chooses a team that keeps headcount inside the shift's min/max band. ✓ Assign all available adds everyone who offered.
Then tick or untick people by hand and Save assignment.

Name a manager

Tick Manager 👑 for whoever leads the shift — they earn 5% more for it, and they're the one who files the shift report.

Watch the coverage bar

Amber means below your minimum at that moment, red means above your maximum. Partial shifts are accounted for, so someone leaving early shows up as a dip.

{# ── 6 · Deliveries ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
6

Plan deliveries

Alongside assigning shifts
Assign a driver

Quickest way: Schedule → 🚚 Deliveries table → pick a name under Assign whole day → Set. A banner counts the days still without a driver.

To split a day between two drivers, go to Deliveries → pick the month → choose a deliverer per client → Save.

The app pre-fills a suggestion: whoever is already saved, then the client's usual driver, then anyone with approved availability that day. Change any of it.

Skip a day

Tick No delivery on a date to black it out — a holiday, say. The calendar stops showing a delivery for that day.

Drop one client from a day

Deliveries → open the day → ✕ on that client's row. This deletes their order for that date.

Who marks it delivered

Drivers tick their own stops on My deliveries & 🍓 runs. You can also do it from the day's delivery page.

Delivery pay: Wed and Thu earn a base fee plus ${{ '%.2f'|format(delivery_transport) }} per stop; other days are per stop only. It lands in salary on its own.

{# ── 7 · Strawberry runs ─────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
7

Assign strawberry runs

Sunday, Wednesday, Friday
Assign from the volunteers

🍓 Purchases → pick the month → tick who does each run → Save assignments

A ✋ marks someone who volunteered for that run when they submitted availability. A banner at the top counts runs nobody is assigned to yet.

Add or remove a date

Sun/Wed/Fri runs appear automatically the first time you open a month. After that the list is yours — use Add a purchase run for an extra date, or ✕ to delete one. Deleted dates stay deleted.

How it pays

Every assigned run reimburses ${{ '%.2f'|format(strawberry_price) }} automatically — it appears under Procurement in salary. Whether "Done?" is ticked makes no difference to pay, so if a run didn't happen, delete the assignment rather than leaving it unticked.

Employees can tick their own runs done on My deliveries & 🍓 runs.

{# ── 8 · Confirm hours ───────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
8

Confirm actual hours worked

After each shift
Approve the shift report

The shift manager files a report with actual start and end times per person, plus stock notes.

Approvals → review the report → Approve

Or set the times yourself

Calendar → 👥 Staff → adjust each person's window → Save assignment

Salary uses actual times where they exist and the planned window otherwise. A shift marked ⚠ unconfirmed on the Salary page still has no actual times.

{# ── 9 · Salary ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
9

Salary & Gusto

At the end of the pay period
Add any pop-up hours

For markets, events, or any work outside the regular production schedule.

Salary → Add pop-up / extra hours: employee, date, times, hourly rate, plus transport (bridge toll, BART).
Leave the rate blank to use the Gusto rate (${{ '%.2f'|format(gusto_rate) }}).

Set the date range and calculate

Salary → set From and To → Calculate

How the hourly rate is worked out per shift:
rate = (${{ '%.2f'|format(piece_rate) }} × daifuku produced) ÷ person-hours on that shift
Manager pay = rate × hours × 1.05. Everyone else = rate × hours.

Read the Gusto column

The highlighted Gusto hours column is the single number to enter per person at ${{ '%.2f'|format(gusto_rate) }}/hr.

Gusto hours = (wages + transport + strawberry reimbursements) ÷ ${{ '%.2f'|format(gusto_rate) }}
Everything is folded in, so there are no separate line items to enter.

Enter it into Gusto

Run payroll and enter each person's Gusto hours at the ${{ '%.2f'|format(gusto_rate) }}/hr base rate. That's it.

{# ── 10 · Invoice ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
10

Invoice clients

Start of the following month
Set the period

Invoice → From and To → Calculate

Pick-ups and pop-ups are left out — they aren't invoiced.

Consignment clients

For consignment, enter what the client reported selling in the Sold column. Leave it blank to bill the full quantity delivered.

Send it through Mercury

Send to Mercury Command copies a ready-made instruction and opens Mercury — paste it and review before sending.

The prompt asks for today's date, Net 15 terms, payment from the checking account, card payment enabled, reminders on, and a memo listing how many pieces went out on each date.

{# ── 11 · Summary ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
11

Month-end summary

Once the month is closed
What the page shows

Summary → pick the month

Sales come from orders priced at each client's rate. Costs are labour — split into production, pop-up, procurement and delivery — plus everything else you record below. Employer payroll tax of {{ '%.2f'|format(tax_rate * 100) }}% is added to labour.

Fix up pop-up sales

A pop-up is assumed to sell everything made less {{ '%g'|format(popup_waste * 100) }}% waste. When one sells unusually well or badly, record what really happened in the Pop-ups section — either pieces sold or an exact amount. Clearing both fields restores the default.

Pull in the cost spreadsheet

Summary → 🔗 Unexpected-cost spreadsheet → paste the sheet's CSV link → Save link → Sync now

Syncing replaces everything previously imported from the sheet, so the spreadsheet stays in charge of its own rows. Anything you typed in by hand is never touched. Imported rows are badged sheet — editing one is pointless, the next sync overwrites it.

Categorise what's left

A banner counts rows still marked Uncategorised, and those rows are tinted. Set the category, amount or note inline and Save categories. Use Add an item for a one-off cost or a bit of income that came from nowhere else.

Read the bottom line

Sales, costs, net and margin for the month. If it looks wrong, the usual causes are unconfirmed shift hours, a client rate that was never set, or a pop-up still on its default.
